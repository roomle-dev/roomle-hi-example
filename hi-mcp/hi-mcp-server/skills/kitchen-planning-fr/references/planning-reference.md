# France Kitchen Planning Reference

Operational reference for [kitchen-planning-fr](../SKILL.md). Source IDs support traceability without requiring bibliography loading. Open the [optional source catalog](source-catalog.md) only for verification, citations or maintenance.

## Evidence boundaries

Research reviewed on 2026-10-09. These are planning checks, not a complete French legal or installation specification. Refresh time-sensitive claims for the actual project. Full NF C 15-100 text, current project-specific accessibility provisions, and product-specific manuals were not inspected in this research.

French-market planning guidance supports familiar workflow principles, not a separate national geometry (FR-S5). Do not infer household habits, a required style, or a gas preference from the country. Imported appliances and retained furniture need exact compatibility checks, not assumptions based on EU marketing.

## French technical and project gates

### Electrical scope and layout reservation

Legrand's professional guide describes the NF C 15-100 series for new or entirely renovated low-voltage installations in France. It reports the 2024 revision, publication on 2024-08-23, and a 12-month overlap with the previous edition ending 2025-08-23; project reference dates include the contract, permit application or prior declaration (FR-S1). Schneider independently confirms a 2024 revision (FR-S2). These summaries do not determine a particular project's legal or contractual obligations.

Ask the electrician to identify the applicable edition, reference date, affected circuits, work scope, protection and testing requirements. Do not assert that replacing cabinets, selling a dwelling, or changing one appliance automatically requires complete rewiring. Conversely, do not use "existing installation" as an exemption from addressing identified hazards or new-work requirements.

Use these source-qualified reservations, not automatic compliance tests:

| Situation | Reservation and qualification |
| --- | --- |
| Kitchen clearly greater than 4 m2 | Legrand reports at least six ordinary sockets on a dedicated kitchen circuit, including four above the worktop. Identify appliance connections separately (FR-S1). |
| Kitchen clearly less than 4 m2 | Legrand reports at least three ordinary sockets. Confirm applicable exception and placement with the electrician; reduced count is not proof of functional adequacy (FR-S1). |
| Exactly 4 m2, uncertain measurement, or open kitchen | The inspected web summary uses less-than/greater-than wording without resolving equality or area definition. Reserve six provisionally and keep applicability unknown until the electrician verifies the full rule. Do not encode a legal threshold from this ambiguity. |
| Hood outlet | Legrand describes an optional additional hood outlet at least 1800 mm above finished floor. This is an outlet position, NOT the hood-to-hob installation clearance (FR-S1). Confirm exact permitted position and product instructions. |
| Hob, oven, dishwasher and other substantial appliances | Record each model, rated load, connection method, required specialized circuit and isolation access. Have the electrician size and verify the installation. Ordinary socket count is not an appliance-circuit allowance. |
| Appliance operation | Verify available supply and simultaneous loads, including retained appliances. Do not assume an existing socket or adapter makes an imported appliance suitable. |

As a conservative planning rule, keep ordinary sockets and trailing leads away from sink and cooking surfaces and do not propose ordinary sockets directly above them. Have the electrician verify exact restrictions and hood exceptions against the applicable standard; this research did not independently verify the full placement clause. Do not import bathroom electrical volumes around a kitchen sink or assert an unsupported universal 600 mm sink/hob separation.

Coordinate outlet positions with credence joints, wall units, drawer backs, appliance depth and accessibility. Do not supply DIY conductor, breaker or wiring instructions as part of a furniture plan. Add task lighting without assuming that a ceiling point alone lights preparation adequately.

### Ventilation, extraction and gas

A hood treats cooking emissions intermittently; dwelling ventilation renews indoor air more generally. Sauter's FAQ explains why a hood cannot replace VMC (FR-S3). This distinction does not prove that every existing French dwelling must receive the same mechanical system.

Survey natural ventilation, individual VMC, collective VMC, air inlets, transfer paths, kitchen extraction terminal, and any combustion appliance. Obtain the relevant design and maintenance information. Do not obstruct vents with cabinets or close a required air path during replacement works.

- Recirculation filters return air to the room; do not count them as outdoor exhaust or as a substitute for moisture removal by dwelling ventilation.
- Dedicated outdoor extraction requires a feasible, authorized duct route and coordinated air supply. Confirm flow, pressure loss, noise, discharge, fire/acoustic provisions and service access professionally.
- Do not connect a powered hood to an unidentified communal duct or VMC terminal. Treat compatibility and permission as unresolved until the responsible ventilation professional and building representative confirm the design. No universal connection prohibition is claimed from the inspected sources.
- Where gas or another combustion appliance is present, obtain professional review of air supply and depressurization risks before changing extraction. Do not infer safety from a closed cabinet drawing.
- Product manuals determine hood clearance, duct arrangement, filters, maintenance and compatibility; generic advice is not installation evidence.

### Copropriete, rented premises and works

These are due-diligence gates, not a sourced statement of voting thresholds or permit law. Determine ownership and permissions before relocating services, cutting walls, changing collective ventilation, penetrating floors, or adding a facade/roof outlet.

Record the reglement de copropriete, syndic response, any required authorization, landlord agreement where relevant, structural assessment, and any mairie/planning-authority requirement. A verbal sales assurance does not resolve these dependencies. Identify who verifies the applicable rules and what evidence is still missing.

For an older building, ask the responsible professional whether surveys for hazardous materials or other pre-work investigations are required before invasive works. Do not diagnose materials from a photograph.

### Rental equipment branch

Service Public's furnished-equipment page, verified 2026-03-06, covers a furnished dwelling used as the tenant's principal residence, including bail mobilite (FR-S4). Its kitchen/meal-related minimum equipment includes:

- Cooking plates.
- An oven OR a microwave, not necessarily both.
- A refrigerator and a freezer, OR a refrigerator with a freezing compartment reaching a maximum temperature of -6 C.
- Dishes sufficient for occupants' meals and cooking utensils.
- A table and seats, storage shelves, and lighting.

The full furnished-dwelling list also covers bedding, bedroom shading and appropriate cleaning equipment. An inventory and detailed furniture condition record accompany the lease. Kitchen planning alone cannot certify the whole dwelling.

Do not add a dishwasher to this minimum list. Do not require luxurious fitted cabinets or apply this list to an owner-occupied home, location nue, or tourist rental. For those branches, identify the applicable requirements separately. The decency overview reviewed during research distinguishes metropolitan and overseas contexts but its detailed equipment sections were not inspected; do not infer that unfurnished rental means "no kitchen obligations."

In a relevant furnished-rental project, separate `regulatory` minimum equipment from `user_must` additions and `preference` upgrades. Verify the current applicable legal basis before final review.

### Accessibility

Confirm whether French housing accessibility provisions apply to the building category, new/existing context, project date, dwelling status and work scope. Ask the responsible professional to supply current requirements and their measurement definitions, including any accessible/evolutive provisions relevant to the project.

The official-partner accessibility portal distinguishes new housing, existing housing and building types, but the inspected page was last updated in 2021 and warns of CCH recodification (FR-S8). It is orientation only, not current numerical kitchen-compliance evidence.

Do not substitute German DIN, US guidance or a generic 1500 mm aisle for the applicable French rule. Separately test the actual user's turning, approach, knee/toe space, reach, handles, controls, sink protection, seated preparation and transfer needs. A user trial supplements, not replaces, applicable requirements.

## Worked checks

### Open-state dishwasher conflict

A 1200 mm closed-front aisle looks generous. A dishwasher projects 600 mm into it and an opposing receiving drawer projects 500 mm. The residual gap is:

```text
1200 - 600 - 500 = 100 mm
```

That is not usable standing/unloading space. It fails this simultaneous-use scenario even if neither closed cabinet footprint overlaps. Move the receiving storage, change opening direction/type or redesign the arrangement; then rerun the test with actual products and the user envelope.

### Unloading travel

Assume four equal batches carried from dishwasher to storage, three return trips, and start at the dishwasher/end at storage. For illustrative measured walkable routes of 1.0 m and 2.5 m:

```text
near_storage_m = (4 + 3) * 1.0 = 7.0
far_storage_m = (4 + 3) * 2.5 = 17.5
difference_per_cycle_m = 17.5 - 7.0 = 10.5
```

Do not use straight-line cabinet distances through open doors. The shorter route wins only if it remains safe and usable; bending and load handling need separate assessment.

### French socket-count boundary

A kitchen is measured at 4.0 m2 with uncertainty, and the seller proposes three ordinary sockets. The inspected manufacturer's less-than/greater-than summary does not settle the boundary. Reserve six provisionally, ask the electrician for the applicable area/count rule, and keep that requirement `unknown`. Even six visible sockets cannot prove circuit or placement compliance.

### Furnished-rental distinction

A principal-residence furnished letting has a hob, microwave and suitable refrigerator/freezing compartment but no conventional oven or dishwasher. The oven-or-microwave requirement is not failed merely because there is no conventional oven; the dishwasher is not on the inspected minimum list. Verify the rest of the equipment, lease scope and dwelling requirements before any compliance conclusion (FR-S4).

### Shared-duct constraint beats preferred island

A proposed island hood depends on an unidentified communal shaft. Do not draw a connection and mark ventilation passed. Keep feasibility/permission `unknown`; compare a service-preserving arrangement and a professionally assessed recirculation option that retains general ventilation. Neither becomes order-review-ready until all applicable hard constraints are resolved.

## Final review questions

Can a user prepare, cook, unload and serve with real occupied/open states? Are French project requirements evidenced rather than presumed? Can installers resolve every service/furniture interface from the documents? Are remaining assumptions visible and incompatible with any claimed readiness? If not, return the proposal to the appropriate earlier stage.
