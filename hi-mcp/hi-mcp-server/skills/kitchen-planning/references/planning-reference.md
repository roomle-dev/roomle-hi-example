# Kitchen Planning Evidence and Constraints

Research checked: 2026-10-09. Scope: residential kitchens, Germany/EU default. This is an original synthesis of public guidance, not a reproduction of a standard, a legal opinion, or an installation specification.

## Evidence Boundaries

AMK, Blum, and IKEA offer useful planning guidance; their editorial advice is not law. Product marketing is not proof of compatibility or universal ergonomic superiority. A named standard is mandatory only where applicable through law, contract, or the project brief; establish that applicability with qualified reviewers.

Short source IDs refer to the optional [source catalog](./source-catalog.md), which holds URLs, publication details, and research notes. Do not load it for routine planning; consult it only for source verification, provenance/citation questions, or guidance updates. Rule classifications and operational qualifications remain in this reference.

The full DIN standard and project-specific appliance installation manuals were not examined. Numerical clearance requirements for a real product therefore remain to be supplied. Verify current applicable requirements and exact product instructions for each project; the research date does not establish a document's technical currency.

Some general advice is too absolute to reuse literally. For example, induction surfaces can remain hot from cookware, lift-up doors still need head/ceiling/reach checks, and handedness does not determine a whole household's best layout. Apply task and product checks rather than repeating marketing assurances.

## Dimensions and How to Use Them

All dimensions below are in mm. Label the rule class and source whenever presenting them to a customer.

| Topic | Starting guidance | Status and measurement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main preparation width | Approximately 900; Blum advises at least 800 for comfortable preparation. | Published ergonomic recommendation [S3], not a statutory minimum. Clear usable work surface, preferably between sink and hob; not centre-to-centre distance. |
| Single-cook working aisle | Explore roughly 1000-1200 initially. | This skill's concept-sizing heuristic, not a sourced legal minimum. Measure the narrowest gap between finished closed projections, then test open appliances and the user. |
| Shared working aisle | Explore roughly 1200-1500 initially. | Concept-sizing heuristic only. Simultaneous work, carry tasks, and passage can need more; extra width also lengthens transfers. |
| Standing preparation height | Start near measured elbow height minus 150. | AMK recommendation [S1]; test in usual footwear with a mock-up. Sink depth and cookware change effective task height. No universal 900-high worktop. |
| Accessible kitchen movement space | The DIN 18040-2 secondary summary describes 1200 x 1200, and 1500 x 1500 for wheelchair use; front clearance depths of 1200 and 1500 respectively. | [S7] is a secondary explanation, not the standard itself. Confirm current applicable provisions, including the wheelchair-specific category, and all other access requirements. These are not universal minimum kitchen dimensions. |
| Dishwasher to dish storage | Comfortable turn or short step where feasible. | Adjacency principle [S4]; no universal maximum distance established by this research. Open-state usability determines success. |
| Hob/oven/fridge landing | Reserve usable nearby surface sized for the actual pan, tray, or grocery load. | Functional planning recommendation. Do not invent a universal legal landing width. The hot-tray route must be safe with the appliance door open. |
| Hood, hob, combustible surfaces | Use exact product and applicable local requirements. | No generic numeric default. Check all applicable instructions; resolving a conflict may require changing products. |
| Cut-out webs, overhangs, supports | Use material/fabricator specifications. | No generic numeric default; material, thickness, cut-outs, support and installation details matter. |
| Drawers, fronts, fillers, service voids | Use selected cabinet/appliance system drawings. | Catalog-specific; never insert an arbitrary filler and assume collision/installation issues are resolved. |

A concept aisle width is not the free width remaining during use. For an illustrative opposing arrangement only, a 1200 aisle minus a 600 dishwasher-door projection minus a 500 drawer projection leaves just 100 between the open fronts. That is not adequate standing space between them. Real doors, racks, handles, body envelopes, and sideways access require actual drawings and task simulation. The numbers here are hypothetical, not product specifications.

## Installation and Usability Checklist

Use the IDs below in the project's constraint register. These are check categories: obtain evidence to establish the exact hard requirement or recommendation for each project. Mark unknowns explicitly.

| ID | Check | Evidence or required outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GEO-01 | Verified geometry | Finished dimensions, out-of-square walls, slopes, trims, levels, and measurement tolerance are represented. No fit claim based only on photos. |
| GEO-02 | Circulation and exit | Doors, clear passages, room access and applicable escape requirements remain usable. Assess appliance-open task states and occupants. |
| GEO-03 | Collision volumes | Check full door/drawer travel, handles, lift fronts, internal pull-outs, adjacent fronts, and head clearance. Closed-box collision tests are insufficient. |
| GEO-04 | Windows and heat | Tap does not prevent required window operation. Window/blind/curtain position, hob heat, drafts, splash exposure and required access are reviewed. |
| GEO-05 | Seating and island | Account for occupied seats, pulled-out chairs, knee space, passage and hot/splash zones. Do not double-count the same area for incompatible simultaneous uses. |
| FIT-01 | Cabinet run and niches | Include end panels, corner solutions, fillers, scribes, wall tolerances and actual appliance niches, not just nominal cabinet widths. |
| FIT-02 | Front and plinth systems | Integrated dishwasher front weight/height, fixed or sliding hinge, plinth sweep, mounting and countertop protection match the exact model. |
| FIT-03 | Refrigerator operation | Required opening angle permits internal drawers/shelves to operate and be removed; wall stand-off, hinges and ventilation are correct. |
| FIT-04 | Hob underside and extraction | Body, induction cooling, required separation, cables, downdraft motor/duct/filter and drawers do not compete for the same volume. |
| FIT-05 | Oven and microwave | Approved niche, ventilation, door travel and shelf reach; hot vessels can be removed safely to a landing surface. A high oven must not require lifting hot trays above the user's safe reach. |
| FIT-06 | Raised appliances | Support structure, fixing, manufacturer approval and reachable controls/racks are verified. Do not put a floor-supported appliance on an improvised shelf. |
| WAT-01 | Sink assembly | Bowl, drainer, clips, tap, waste, trap, hoses, drawers and bins fit together; servicing remains possible. |
| WAT-02 | Water and drainage | Qualified installer verifies supplies, pressure, hot-water compatibility, drainage route/levels, appliance hose limits and required backflow protection. Proximity alone proves none of these. |
| WAT-03 | Shutoff and leak access | Isolation, connections and leak-protection equipment remain accessible as required; no pinched hoses or inaccessible maintenance points. |
| WAT-04 | Special water equipment | Boiling/chilled/carbonated/filter systems have approved taps, tank/filter/cylinder space, ventilation, power, maintenance and child protection. |
| ELE-01 | Electrical design | Qualified electrician verifies available supply, connected loads, circuits, protective devices, earthing, isolation and socket installation under applicable rules. |
| ELE-02 | Socket and cable placement | Plan for all fixed and small appliances, lighting and powered fittings. Keep cords out of wet/hot areas and across no circulation route; verify plugs do not consume a prohibited niche/service volume. |
| ELE-03 | No invented wet-area rule | Do not transpose bathroom electrical zones into a kitchen or assert a blanket socket-to-sink distance without an applicable source. Obtain the electrician's project-specific layout. |
| GAS-01 | Gas equipment | Approved professional checks supply, isolation, installation, ventilation and appliance-specific combustible clearances. No DIY connection instructions. |
| VEN-01 | Extraction choice | Establish exhaust or recirculation early. Check hood capture at the actual hob, installed airflow, noise, filter access, duct dimensions, bends, outlet and replacement-air strategy. |
| VEN-02 | Recirculation limits | Recirculation normally does not remove cooking moisture outdoors and is not a substitute for necessary room ventilation. Check system-specific requirements. |
| VEN-03 | Combustion interaction | Exhaust plus a room-air-dependent fireplace/heater triggers professional pressure/backdraft review with the responsible chimney sweep and ventilation specialist. Do not assume a window switch alone makes the system safe. |
| VEN-04 | Building permissions | Check landlord/owners' association approvals, facade penetrations, fire separations, communal shafts and whole-dwelling ventilation interactions. |
| TOP-01 | Worktop structure | Fabricator verifies cut-out spacing, edge webs, joint locations, supports, spans, overhangs, thermal limits and installation method. |
| TOP-02 | Water and steam protection | Seal/protect cut-outs, joints, sink area and dishwasher edges as specified; account for automatic-opening dishwasher steam. |
| STR-01 | Fixings and stability | Suitable wall fixings, rails, suspended loads, tall-unit restraint, island support and loaded drawers. Check floor/underfloor services before drilling. |
| STR-02 | Structural alterations | No wall removal or structural drilling assumption without qualified assessment and required permissions. |
| USE-01 | Preparation and landings | Real unobstructed preparation and grocery/hot-vessel landing space remains after permanent countertop items are placed. |
| USE-02 | Unloading | Dishwasher, rack, receiving storage and user can occupy needed positions together; no stepping over the door or excessive reaching. |
| USE-03 | Waste and hygiene | Scrape/sort/load route works; bins can be removed and cleaned. Separate chemicals from food/clean items; provide cleanable food-contact surfaces and drying space. |
| USE-04 | Storage capacity and loads | Actual inventory fits internally with chosen organizers and runner ratings. Record spare capacity if requested; do not invent a mandatory percentage. |
| USE-05 | Multi-user tasks | Test concurrent cooking, dish handling, drinks access and table setting; identify crossings and bottlenecks. |
| ACC-01 | Accessibility | Confirm required standard/category; check turning and approach spaces, knee/toe clearance, seated reach, controls, grip/force, task height and protected hot pipes. Test with the actual user/mobility aid. |
| SAFE-01 | Children and burns | Secure knives/chemicals, review reachable controls and hot-water dispensing, prevent pot-handle exposure to traffic/seating. Induction still has residual heat; child locks do not replace supervision. |
| SAFE-02 | Flooring and edges | Review slip/trip risk, thresholds, protrusions, sharp corners and cleanability. Do not let loose mats create an access hazard. |
| LGT-01 | Lighting | Illuminate preparation, sink and hob without the user casting a working shadow. Check glare, contrast, controls and usable light beneath cabinets; lighting drivers remain serviceable. |
| ENV-01 | Comfort and maintenance | Compare actual appliance noise, energy/water data, extractor filter costs, cleanability, material heat/moisture resistance and repairability. Verify current product labels. |
| SRV-01 | Replacement access | Appliances can be isolated, removed and replaced after flooring/worktops are installed; filters, traps, drivers and fixings can be serviced. |
| ORD-01 | Complete order scope | Include appliances, cabinets, fronts, hinges, runners, handles, fillers, panels, plinths, feet, rails, supports, worktops, cut-outs, lighting, extraction, fittings, delivery, installation and service work. |
| ORD-02 | Release gate | Final survey, product/manual revisions, costs, permissions and responsible professional checks match the exact proposed order. Obtain explicit order authorization. |

## Worked Arrangement: Pattern, Not Template

An illustrative working run can be:

`[dish/cutlery drawers] [dishwasher] [sink] [prep drawers + clear worktop] [hob/cookware]`

Daily cups/glasses can occupy comfortably reachable storage above the dish bank or another nearby accessible location. Waste can be under the sink or in a separate nearby pull-out if loading, preparation and bin removal all work. Do not consume the only preparation drawer space without a replacement.

This pattern places clean storage on one side of cleanup and preparation on the other. It is neither a complete kitchen nor a mandatory sequence: refrigerator/pantry, oven, landing surfaces, end conditions and services must still be located. Mirror or split the run when the room and users benefit. A hob at the run's exposed end still needs its verified clearances and usable landing arrangement.

For an L-layout, a drawer around the corner from the dishwasher may be physically close but impossible to open during unloading. For a galley, opposing dish drawers may work or may block the user's standing position. The decisive check is the open-state task, not the diagram's apparent neatness.

### Transparent Travel Example

Suppose two otherwise feasible options each need four storage transfers per dishwasher cycle. Assume three return legs to the dishwasher before the final transfer, one cycle daily, and equal one-way path lengths in either direction:

- Option A: 1.0 m per leg -> `(4 + 3) * 1.0 = 7.0 m` per cycle.
- Option B: 2.5 m per leg -> `(4 + 3) * 2.5 = 17.5 m` per cycle.
- Difference: 10.5 m per cycle under these assumptions, excluding any final return and other kitchen tasks.

These are illustrative distances and batch counts, not research results. Do not extrapolate time or lifetime savings without measurements. A comfortable turn may produce almost no walking but still involve awkward reaching; report that separately. An option with an obstructed unloading path is infeasible regardless of its calculated distance.

## Maintenance and Unverified Requirements

The checklist is deliberately broad but not exhaustive. Actual regulatory requirements, permitted clearances, and compatibility depend on the site, products and users. Obtain the current applicable primary sources, exact installation manuals and qualified reviews before converting any unresolved checklist item into a pass.

Revisit this reference when products, material systems, regulations or source guidance change. Keep the general workflow vendor-neutral; replace examples with the project's authorized catalog without losing the open-door, capacity, accessibility and safety checks.
